## Your Operating Model by Company Size

### STARTUPS (Pre-Series B to Series B)

**Your role:**
- **Tech:** I drive it. Strategy, architecture, hiring, execution. You own the outcomes.
- **Other functions:** I'm the sounding board. I provide guardrails, direction, and facilitate problem-solving conversations. I don't run it, but I shape it.

**Time commitment:** 20-30 hours/week (significant)

**Equity:** 0.5-1.5% (commensurate with operational role)

**What you're solving:**
- Tech scaling (infrastructure, architecture, tech debt)
- Tech hiring and culture
- Product-tech alignment
- Go-to-market and scaling operations

**Your actual title:** Board Member + VP Operations (or Chief Operating Officer, depending on structure)

### GROWTH-STAGE/IPO-READY (Series D+, Pre-IPO, Recently IPO'd)

**Your role:**
- **One critical function:** R&D, Infrastructure, Scaling Operations, or similar. You own strategy, decisions, and outcomes.
- **Strategic influence:** Major business decisions (pivots, bets, acquisitions) benefit from your input.

**Time commitment:** 10-15 hours/week (focused)

**Equity:** 0.25-0.5% (board + strategic advisor)

**What you're solving:**
- De-risking major R&D bets (which will win, which won't)
- Infrastructure and scaling bottlenecks
- Operational efficiency and leverage
- Strategic decisions on emerging opportunities

**Your actual title:** Board Member + Advisor (Tech/Operations)

### LARGE ENTERPRISE (S&P 500—Future Aspiration)

**Your role:**
- Bring entrepreneurial operator mentality to established boards
- Rigorous enterprise-level tech understanding + willingness to challenge status quo
- Strategic advisor on critical tech/operations decisions

**Time commitment:** 5-10 hours/week (strategic)

**Compensation:** Cash compensation + equity (board seat)

**Why this stage:** Build credibility at growth-stage/IPO companies first. S&P 500 boards value operators with experience scaling established brands.

**Your actual title:** Board Member

## Your Track Record

### Censeo → Mercer Acquisition (14 years, organic growth + successful exit)

**The story (use this in face-to-face conversations):**

I built Censeo from scratch—no VC funding, no investors. I funded tech development through consulting revenue while building trusted vendor relationships with enterprise clients.

Over 14 years, we grew organically to serve 500+ enterprise clients: UPS, Sanofi, Deloitte, and hundreds of mom-and-pop consultants. We built 360 MultiRater Survey, TalentSim, KnowledgeTrack, Employee Opinion Survey platforms.

I retained every employee through the entire 14-year run. When we sold to Mercer, even though employees didn't have equity stakes, I distributed a portion of the sale proceeds to 20 employees in proportion to their years with the company.

**Why this matters for your board positioning:**
- **Organic growth** (not a quick flip, but sustainable business)
- **Earned trust** (S&P 500 clients, mom-and-pop consultants, same vendor for 14 years)
- **Self-funded** (discipline: revenue pays for development, not burning VC money)
- **Employee care** (retained everyone, shared the exit)
- **Long-term thinking** (14 years of patience, not a sprint)
- **Integrity** (took care of people, not just shareholders)

This is the story that shows you're not here for the board seat prestige—you're here because you build sustainable businesses and take care of people.

### Learning Experiences (Companies that didn't exit):

**Quaeris, JourneyLabs, EDA, Halestreet advisory:**
- Scaled operations, improved architecture, optimized costs
- Learned market fit and adoption challenges firsthand
- Built operational excellence but faced market adoption barriers

**Why this matters:**
- You can spot when a company has execution but lacks market adoption
- You understand the difference between scaling (Censeo: 14 years, 500+ clients) and unsustainable growth (others: great tech, no market fit)
- You won't BS about "growth" that isn't tied to real market demand

## How I Think About Technology (face-to-face talking point, not LinkedIn copy)

**Why this isn't in the LinkedIn About section:** it's true and you believe it completely, but stated cold on a profile it reads as generic — nearly every competent CTO says some version of "tech should serve the business, not chase trends." It doesn't differentiate you in writing. It lands much better as an answer to a direct question in conversation, where you can back it with a real example.

**The talking point, for when someone asks "how do you think about technology decisions?":**

Technology is a means to an end. I don't bring in the latest, sexiest, or most trendy tech. I bring in tech that solves a business problem or helps achieve a business goal — keeping time, complexity, and cost in mind.

If I don't understand a technology deeply, I'll bring in someone who does. But they walk *our* path, not their own. No ego, no tangents. Alignment on outcomes.

**The genuinely useful part, worth leading with:** the second point — how you manage outside expertise and keep it accountable to outcomes rather than its own agenda — is actually a general governance principle, not a tech-specific one. It applies just as well to legal, financial, or regulatory expertise a board brings in. Frame it that way if the conversation is about governance broadly, not just tech.
