## How You Add Value (The 5 Points, In Your Voice) — expanded/stale version

### 1. I spot the real bottleneck—not the obvious one

Most leaders optimize what's visible. They miss the thing actually costing them.

Example: A founder thought their scaling problem was code quality. It wasn't. It was lock contention in the database—10 workers fighting over the same rows. One architectural fix: 12x speedup.

I find that. And I fix it.

### 2. I'm not here to validate—I'll challenge you

You don't need another yes-man. You need someone who tells you when you're wrong.

If you're betting $5M on an R&D initiative that looks like hype instead of adoption, I'll tell you. If you're thinking too small on a market opportunity, I'll push you.

I've pitched to boards, PE, and angels—I know what investors look for and what founders need to communicate. I can help you tell your story compellingly in layman's terms. But I'll also push back when strategy isn't sound.

I won't always be right. But I'll be honest.

### 3. I've been there—built, scaled, and learned from failure

I've built 4 companies from scratch. One successful exit (Censeo to Mercer). The others taught me what kills companies:
- Shipping mediocre products
- Confusing scaling with sustainability
- Chasing adoption instead of building product-market fit
- Optimizing the wrong thing

I can see these patterns in your company. I can warn you before you hit them.

### 4. I own outcomes—I'm not on the sidelines

I'm not an advisor who makes recommendations and disappears. I'm betting my equity and credibility that this works.

If tech scaling fails, I failed. If the R&D bet doesn't pay off, I own it. This skin in the game means I'm all-in on your success.

### 5. I drive results through rigor, not politics

I test. I iterate. I measure what matters. I don't BS about progress that isn't there.

If something isn't working, I admit it immediately and pivot. If it's working, I can tell you exactly why and how to scale it.

## Your Technology Philosophy — expanded version

**The core principle:** Technology is a means to an end, not an end in itself.

**What this means:**

I don't bring in the latest, sexiest, or most trendy tech.

I bring in tech that:
- Solves a business problem
- Helps achieve a business goal
- Respects time, complexity, and cost constraints

**If I don't know it, I find someone who does.** I'm humble enough to admit the limits of my knowledge.

**But they walk our path.** I bring in deep experts—architects, infrastructure specialists, domain experts. But they're aligned with business outcomes, not pursuing technical purity.

No ego. No tangents. Alignment on what matters.

**Examples of this philosophy:**

**What I would NOT do:**
- Rewrite in the latest language because it's trendy (unless it solves a real problem)
- Over-engineer infrastructure for hypothetical scale
- Adopt complex patterns because they're intellectually interesting
- Let engineers disappear into technical rabbit holes

**What I WOULD do:**
- Choose boring, proven tech that solves the problem fastest
- Build only what you need now, architect for what you'll need
- Make infrastructure decisions based on business constraints (time, cost, complexity)
- Bring in specialists and make sure they're aligned with business goals

## How to Position Yourself to Founders (canned Q&A scripts)

> I run critical operations. For startups, that's tech + sounding board on everything else. For larger companies, that's owning one strategic function—R&D, infrastructure, scaling.
>
> I spot bottlenecks others miss. I make bets that move the needle. And I own outcomes—not advising from the sidelines, but betting my equity that this works.
>
> I'm Ajit Jain, not a social board member with a Rolodex. I'm the operational brain.

### When they ask "How do you make decisions?"

> I test hypotheses. I measure what matters. I admit when something isn't working and pivot immediately.
>
> I won't tell you what you want to hear. I'll tell you what's true.

### When they ask "Why technology?"

> Technology is a tool to solve business problems and achieve business goals. I don't chase trends. I choose what works—boring, proven, effective.
>
> If I don't know something deeply, I bring in someone who does. But they walk our path, not their own. Alignment on outcomes.
