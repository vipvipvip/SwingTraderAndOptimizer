## Tax map

Your federal tax is low under every strategy: $49,000–88,000 over 35 years. Health-premium and survivor effects matter more than the bracket math.

- **Conversion headroom.** Filling the 12% bracket allows about $110,000–121,000 of conversions a year in 2027–2030. Keeping income under the ACA cliff allows about $54,000–59,000 a year.
- **ACA cliff (the biggest pitfall).** Under the plan as written, MAGI is $131,000–155,000 in 2027–2030, all above the cliff (about $86,600, indexed). That forfeits an estimated $14,000 of subsidy in 2027 and $6,000 a year in 2028–2030, about **$32,000 total**. This is more than the plan's lifetime federal tax. Roth conversions, capital gains and IRA withdrawals all count toward ACA income.
- **IRMAA.** No Medicare surcharge in any tested strategy. Peak MAGI is about $166,000 (2032, ACA-aware plan), below the $218,000 first tier.
- **Senior bonus deduction.** A gets $6,000 in 2028 only, the one year he is 65+ before it expires. B turns 65 after it ends.
- **Social Security tax torpedo.** This only bites if the IRA survives into the Social Security years. Without conversions, once both benefits start (about $73,000), half of Social Security alone puts provisional income past $32,000. Each extra $1 of IRA withdrawal then adds up to $1.85 of taxable income: about a 22% effective rate inside the 12% bracket.
- **RMDs.** RMDs start at 75 (A in 2038, B in 2042), not 73. With either conversion strategy, the IRAs are empty before then, so RMDs never apply. Without conversions, A's first RMD is about $10,700 in 2038, rising to about $64,000 of IRA withdrawals by 2040.
- **Widow's penalty.** Without conversions, if A dies in 2040, B pays about $30,000 more lifetime tax ($117,600 vs. $87,400) because single brackets apply to the same IRA income. Converting early removes this risk.

What each test shows:

- **Inflation.** The $26,250 coupon is fixed. After 10 years of 5% inflation it buys what $16,100 buys today, a 39% loss. Social Security COLAs don't help in 2027–2030 because no benefit has started yet, so the gap years carry the full inflation risk.
- **Sequence risk.** The crash does the most damage because 2027–2030 withdrawals come entirely from equity accounts. The bond bullet sits untouched in the IRA, so nothing cushions those sales.
- **Survivor.** Depletion doesn't move because the survivor's lower spending roughly offsets the loss of the $25,000 benefit. The tax effect is in the Tax map.

## Strategy critique

Two of your five strategies need changing: the conversion target and the withdrawal order. The bond structure needs rethinking. The other two work.

| Strategy | Verdict | Dollar impact | Better alternative |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Roth conversions to top of 12%, 2027–2030 | Works for tax, loses on health premiums | Saves about $38,000 lifetime tax vs. no conversions, but costs about $32,000 in lost ACA subsidy | Convert up to the ACA cliff through 2031 (about $54,000–59,000/yr), then fill 12% in 2032–2033. Lifetime tax is $48,700 with no subsidy loss. |
| Drain HSA first | No benefit | Spends your only fully tax-free, tax-free-growing account first | Spend taxable first, HSA second, Roth last. Taxable sales now carry almost no gain, so they keep ACA income low. |
| 10-year Treasury bullet held to 2036 | Doesn't do what it is meant to | Protects nothing in the gap years: equities are sold while bonds sit; 39% real loss of the coupon at 5% inflation | A 2027–2034 Treasury/TIPS ladder in the IRA, spent down as rungs mature. That is an actual bond tent. |
| Asset location (Treasuries in IRA) | Correct but small | At 0–12% federal brackets and no Florida tax, the tax drag being avoided is small | Keep it, but don't count on the 0.75%/yr "tax alpha" figure. It assumes higher tax rates than yours. |
| Social Security: A at 68, B at 67 | Not tested | n/a | Needs your SSA estimates at 62 / FRA / 70 (see Open questions). |

**Why the bond bullet isn't a bond tent.** In the Kitces/Pfau approach, you spend bonds first in the risky early years, so your equity share rises over time. Your plan spends equity accounts in 2027–2030 and holds the bonds to 2036, so your equity share falls just when a crash would hurt most. Converting to the 12% bracket also moves most of the bullet into the Roth by 2030.

**HSA, given $100,000 of receipts.** Your receipts exceed the balance, so every HSA dollar can come out tax-free at any time. Neither the age-65 penalty waiver nor the Medicare and long-term care pathways is needed, though new Medicare premiums and qualified LTC premiums will add to your reimbursable pool. The real deadline is death: a spouse inherits the HSA as their own, but a non-spouse heir pays income tax on the whole balance. So plan to empty it before the second death, not first.

Shoebox checklist:

1. Scan every receipt, EOB and pharmacy record since the HSA opened. Expenses before the HSA's establishment date don't count.
2. Build a ledger: date, provider, patient, amount, and a link to each scanned file. Total it, and confirm none of it was already reimbursed or deducted.
3. Withdraw up to the ledger total as needed. Note each withdrawal against specific ledger lines.
4. Report distributions on Form 8889 each year, and keep the ledger and files for as long as the HSA has a balance plus at least 3 years.
