# Board Governance Dossier

Max is a founder (Censeo, sold to Mercer in 2011) pursuing nonprofit and then corporate board seats. A dossier exists to answer one question: **what governance gap does this board have, and is Max credibly the person who fills it?** Research that doesn't end at that answer is just a summary of a website.

Read `references/max-profile.md` before writing Section 4 — it holds the only facts about Max you may use.

## Ground rules (these come from Max directly)

1. **No unverified numbers.** Every figure in the dossier carries its source and the fiscal year it covers (e.g. "Form 990, FY2024, Part I Line 12"). If a number can't be found in a primary source, write "not found" — never estimate or carry over a figure from a previous dossier or the user's notes without re-checking it. A wrong number said to a trustee costs more credibility than no number.
2. **Never invent facts about Max.** Censeo's acquisition valuation is undocumented; don't state or imply one. Use only what is in `references/max-profile.md` or what Max says in the conversation.
3. **Language:** his philosophy is "eyes in, hands on" — never "hands out" or "hands off". Never call him an "operator"; it signals execution, not judgment. Frame his value as decisions made, capital allocated, stakeholder outcomes — not tools or tasks.
4. **Gaps come from the actual roster**, not sector stereotypes. Look up who is really on the board and what they do before claiming a gap exists. If the roster isn't public, say so and label the gap analysis as inferred.

## Step 1: Classify the organization

The sources differ by type, so identify it first (search if unclear):

- **Nonprofit (501(c))** → see `references/sources-nonprofit.md` (Form 990 via ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer, Candid/GuideStar, Charity Navigator).
- **Public company** → see `references/sources-public-company.md` (proxy statement DEF 14A, 10-K, 8-K via SEC EDGAR).
- **Private company / startup / advisory board** → see `references/sources-private.md` (limited data; lean on leadership pages, press, funding news, LinkedIn).

Read the matching reference file, then research. Use several searches and fetch the primary documents themselves rather than relying on search snippets or aggregator summaries. Check for news in the last 12 months (leadership changes, lawsuits, expansions, funding, layoffs) — recent events often reveal the real board tension faster than filings.

## Step 2: Build the four sections

### Section 1 — Fiduciary Snapshot (financial health)
Revenue vs. expenses trend (ideally 3 years, so one-off years don't mislead), funding/revenue mix and concentration risk, efficiency or margin signals, reserves/cash position. Each figure: value, source, year.

### Section 2 — Board Composition & Gaps
List the current board (name, primary profession/affiliation, committee roles if known). Tally the skill mix. Identify gaps against what the organization's situation actually demands — technology, cybersecurity/data privacy, M&A, scaling, finance, risk. Note board size, independence, chair/CEO split, and any announced vacancies or term limits.

### Section 3 — Operational Strain
What is the organization straining against right now? Leadership stability (CEO/ED tenure and turnover), demand pressure in its sector locally, infrastructure and technology investment vs. deferral, regulatory/compliance exposure, growth or contraction signals.

### Section 4 — Strategic Entry Alignment
This is the payoff section and must connect to Max.

- **The core governance tension** — one or two sentences: the single biggest balance point this board faces.
- **Fit assessment** — map the gap from Section 2 to specific, documented elements of Max's background (from `max-profile.md`). Name the committee(s) he'd most credibly serve on. Be honest: rate fit as Strong / Moderate / Weak and say why. A weak fit is a useful answer — it saves him a season of networking in the wrong room.
- **Opening line (lead with intent)** — default to transparency about his goal, which disarms the "interviewer" vibe, e.g. *"I'm exploring board service here in Orlando — from your seat, what does this board need most right now?"* Tailor it to the organization.
- **Peer hook (held in reserve)** — one observation he can use *if* the conversation turns to operations or strategy. Keep numbers out of the opener; reciting a dollar figure to someone you just met reads as an audit. The hook should ideally bridge to his experience naturally, without sounding like a pitch.
- **Questions to ask** — 2–3 open questions that let the trustee/executive talk and reveal whether the gap is real.

## Step 3: Verification block

End the dossier with a short **"Verify before use"** list: any figure older than the most recent filing, anything sourced only from secondary sites, inferred gaps, and names/titles that may have changed. Include the date the dossier was compiled.

## Output

Deliver the dossier as a document Max can keep and revisit before events (use the environment's default document format), titled `[Organization] — Board Governance Dossier`, with the four sections, verification block, and a source list with links. Keep it scannable: he'll reread it in a parking lot ten minutes before walking in. In chat, give a 3–4 sentence summary: the core tension, fit rating, and the one thing to remember.

If Max is preparing for a specific event, also add a brief **Event prep** note: host names (ED/CEO, board chair, event chair), dress code if stated by the host, and the local news context worth knowing. See `references/event-prep.md`.

## Data-to-dialogue reference

Use this to turn a finding into a peer observation rather than a question that sounds like an audit:

| Finding | Underlying board tension | Peer observation sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| Very high program ratio / lean overhead | Little margin for back-office failure; systems and security underfunded | "Impressive how much goes straight to programs — must be a tightrope keeping the back office secure on that lean an overhead." |
| Heavy facilities or real-estate base | Fixed costs, liability, long-horizon asset decisions | "Running a physical campus is a different beast from a program you can scale digitally — the fixed baseline alone makes asset strategy a board-level issue." |
| Large subsidy / fee-assistance figure | Mission depends on stable philanthropy to close the gap | "That level of assistance is great for the mission — it really puts weight on multi-year partnerships as a buffer." |
| Government-grant concentration | Funding volatility, policy risk | "With that much public funding, I'd imagine diversification is a standing conversation at the board level." |
| Recent CEO/ED transition | Board is resetting strategy and oversight | "Leadership transitions are usually when a board rethinks what it needs around the table." |
| Digital transformation / new platform underway | Board may lack anyone who can pressure-test tech spend | "Big platform investments are hard to govern if no one on the board has lived through one." |
| Public company: thin tech/cyber expertise in proxy skills matrix | Oversight gap on a material risk | "Cyber and data oversight keep moving up the board agenda — it's interesting how boards are staffing for it." |
